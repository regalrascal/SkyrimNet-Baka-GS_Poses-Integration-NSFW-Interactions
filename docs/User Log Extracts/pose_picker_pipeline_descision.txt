Agreed — and your reasoning lands exactly on the architectural line that matters: the decision model earns its keep when it can be handed better-formalized inputs than the generalist already has (numbers, decorators, cheap Papyrus pulls that can carry decision weight). A pose choice is the opposite case — it's a semantic judgment made of exactly the context the first LLM is already holding. Handing it to the picker means paying to strip context down to a thin pack and asking a lesser model to reconstruct the judgment. Your "robs choice context rather than sharpening the decision" is the correct diagnosis, and it matches the planner's own instinct — the decision API was always a candidate, never a requirement (B11).

So the ruling this makes: retire the pose picker's decision-model leg — and here's the good news about what actually survives, because this isn't a deletion, it's a demotion:

The picker's build wasn't wasted. The decision-chain plumbing (context packs, callbacks, the shipped template) works and is proven. It becomes a pattern on the shelf for the cases where it genuinely fits — and your criterion just defined those cases: pure decorator/number inputs with decidable weight. If a future feature needs a numeric decision (a fight-flight intensity roll, a yes/no consent gate keyed to hard state, an arousal-thresholded branch), the machinery is sitting there.
The Sanguine pipeline simplifies to your steps 1–4 plus the consideration, which already did the work. The consideration call picks the category and the member in one pass with full context — which was always the alternative shape, and is now the shape. The thin context pack stops being a decision input and stops existing as a bottleneck.
The dialogue/emergent lane was never on the picker (your 137f(d) ruling, now standing permanently rather than deferred) — so it loses nothing. The 2.3a wording fix and the pending exec fix are the whole cure for that lane.
